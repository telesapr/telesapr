# Changelog

What changed in each release of TeleSAPR, newest first. The text of a version's section is the
body of its GitHub release (`make release`, `doc/releasing.md`).

Write an entry in the commit that makes the change, under `## [Unreleased]`, in the group it
belongs to: `Added`, `Changed`, `Fixed` or `Removed`. At the version bump, rename `Unreleased`
to `## [X.Y.Z] - YYYY-MM-DD` and put a new empty `## [Unreleased]` above it.

## [Unreleased]

## [0.6.2] - 2026-11-08

### Added
- Fillets and chamfers of a pocket's rim turn its inside corners. Picking two edges of the rim that meet there, three, or all of them, of a pocket cut into a curved face such as a dish, used to be refused ("the fillet's mitre doesn't reach the crease"): the two now end where the ball touches the sharp edge between the pocket's walls, and a corner patch, tangent to both and to the face they share, fills between them. In a flat face the two are mitred instead, as OpenCASCADE does. Any one, two or three edges of a pocket's rim in a flat face, which were refused ("an edge at the end of the fillet runs back along it"), are rounded or chamfered too.
- AI connection: a long run_script reports how far it has got while it runs, so a client no longer waits minutes with nothing to show, or gives up, while a large model regenerates. A client that asked for progress is told the group being regenerated ("regenerating g007-plate, 3 of 12") and the last line printed. A client that shows logs gets each line the script prints as it is printed. The browser's `agent-client.js` `run` takes a callback for the same reports. `telesapr-cli mcp` and the window must be the same version (the protocol is now 6).
- `telesapr-cli mcp --record <file>` appends every message the AI client sends to a file, one per line, in the window mode and with `--headless`: a session with a model to send to the developers or to keep as a test.
- AI connection: get_drawing and measure return, beside their text, the same as data a program can read (MCP's structured content, with an output schema): the groups with how they solved, their volume and bounds, the requests with their points, the constraints with their values, the faces and edges of a solid; the volume, centre of mass, bounds, soundness, gaps and walls measured. A client, an evaluation or a test no longer parses the prose. Scripts: `Doc.Description`, `Group.Description`, `Group.SolidDescription`; in a browser, `agent-client.js` has `described` and `measured`. `telesapr-cli mcp` and the window must be the same version (the protocol is now 6).
- AI connection: get_drawing with `solid` lists the faces and edges of the solid up to a group (the active one by default): each face with its ID, name, shape (flat with its normal, cylinder or cone with its axis, sphere, or curved) and a point on it, each edge with the name `Solid().Edge` takes, its shape, length and ends, and its two faces; at most 40 faces and 60 edges. A model picking edges to fillet or a face to sketch on no longer has to find them by trial scripts. Scripts: `Group.DescribeSolid`. `telesapr-cli mcp` and the window must be the same version (the protocol is now 5).
- AI connection: get_drawing with `script` returns the whole drawing as the Go script that builds it again, as File > Export > Script (.go) writes it, the quickest way for a model to see how a drawing it didn't make is built.

### Changed
- A script's run-time error names the statement that failed, in plain words: `script:2:1: index out of range` and the line quoted below it, where it said `panic: reflect: slice index out of range`. The console, `telesapr-cli` and the AI tools all show it.
- AI connection: render sends a line of text with the picture (the view, its size, the group drawn, the section, and the solid's bounds in mm), and with only `width` or only `height` given the other side follows at 4:3. `telesapr-cli mcp` and the window must be the same version.
- AI connection: after a script was stopped, run_script says that the interpreter started afresh and the variables of earlier calls are gone (it used to say they were still defined). The primer's example rounds the plate's edges, its "Checking the result" lines are real calls, and it no longer sends a model to `SolidEdges` (a chamfer group's) for an extrusion's edges. The error of a call made on a group of the wrong kind says "an extrude" for "a extrude".

## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-06

### Added
- Mechanisms (experimental): a link group's part can be set to move in a mechanism (its screen's row part: moves in a mechanism, or `LinkGroup.SetMoves` in scripts). The flagged links in a row in the list of groups are solved as one system, so a mate in one moves the others and a loop of parts, as a four-bar linkage, closes, keeping its one degree of freedom. Unflagged links and everything before are held as before. The flag is saved (`link-moves`). A point of a mechanism's part dragged in the window or moved by a script (`Entity.MoveTo`, `MoveBy`) is pulled along the mechanism's freedom, so a hinged bar pulled by its end turns about its hinge instead of snapping back; bounds stop the pull; a part its mates leave free to slide slides where it is pulled, rather than turning about its origin. Assembly > Mate Hinge, Driven and Mate Concentric, Driven (`Assembly.HingeDriven`, `ConcentricDriven`) make a hinge or a concentric mate whose turn or slide is a new parameter, `angle` or `travel`, for a motion study to drive (a driven hinge of two edges, or of a pin and its hole); `SetRange` keeps it between two values. A turn about an axis (a screw mate's, a hinge's) can be a minimum or a maximum, and Minimum or Maximum Dimension on a dimension a parameter drives puts the bound beside it instead of dropping the drive.
- A fillet or chamfer round a circular edge whose foot runs past its flat face into a side where the solid goes on beyond it, as round a rod through a plate with a ledge below the plate's side or a tab standing off it, is made: cut back by the side's plane as where nothing is beyond, what lies past the side left as it was; one whose foot runs into a side with holes in it, as a cross bore; and one whose foot runs into a flat side that leans to the face, as a drafted plate's, cut back along the side's own plane. All three were refused as running off the face.
- Fillet and chamfer of an edge whose end runs off a face onto a tightly rounded one, as a wide chamfer of a band's side on a cone past the band's round top, are made: the rounded face is carried on past its edge by its tangent plane, a flat face beside it that counts as the same face, and the blend's end runs onto it. The rounded face carried on as its own curve came round under the blend's end again, and the blend was refused as running off "the faces on both sides".
- Sweep Along Path takes a sharp corner next to a cubic along a round profile, such as a rod up a line and off at a right angle along a curve, and along a diamond whose vertex lies on the corner's axis: they were refused, the diamond's unless the cubic was straight. The two sides touch where the profile crosses the corner's axis, and the joins now meet there. A turn of 150° with the cubic bent sharply the same way is still refused along a round profile.
- A rib's top edges can be rounded after its foot was filled, in a fillet of their own: where an upright or a slope's edge runs down onto the foot's fillets, the corner is set back and filled by patches tangent to the three fillets, the faces and the floor, the same corner the nine edges rounded in one edit give. Before, such picks were refused ("round that edge too", or "the ball the fillet rolls doesn't touch the face").
- Shell opens a drafted extrusion's round side at an inside corner beside a flat side, as a round lobe on a drafted block's edge: the inside wall under the flat side stops a little short of the corner (on a lobe of radius 4, about a fiftieth of the thickness at small drafts, up to 0.25 at 10°; the volume within 0.22% of walls reaching the corner), and the group says how far in a note on its screen. It was refused. Scripts: `Note()` on a group says how its solid differs from what it asks for.
- Shell opens a drafted extrusion's round side at an inside corner beside a round side kept too, as a round lobe on a drafted disc's rim: the wall under the rim stops a little short of the corner as beside a flat side, set further back where the corner moves far going up (on a lobe of radius 3 on a disc of radius 10, from 0.01 short up to nearly 1 at a draft of 8° and a thickness of 0.5; the volume within 1.3% of walls reaching the corner), and the group's note says how far. It was refused.
- Shell opens a drafted extrusion's round side at an inside corner beside a hollow round side kept, as a round lobe in a drafted crescent's hollow: the wall under the hollow side stops a little short of the corner (0.01 to 0.15 for a lobe of radius 2 in hollows of radius 4 to 9), the inside following the hollow's circle outside it, and the group's note says how far. It was refused.
- Shell opens a lathe's or revolve's curved face where the solid comes back within the thickness outside it, as across a slot narrower than the thickness beside a cylinder taken away, or a step that rises again: the inside runs out past the face by less, down to an eighth of the thickness. It was refused. So is a curved face taken away beside a cone, a sphere or a torus taken away too.
- Shell opens a lathe's or revolve's curved face at an inside corner beside a cone, a sphere or a torus, as a cylinder standing on a coned or rounded shoulder taken away with its top: the wall under the shoulder keeps its thickness and ends square to it at the corner. It was refused. So is one beside a flat step narrower than the thickness with nothing rising beyond it.
- Features > Broken References mends a group's axis, direction or plane that a damaged file lacks, as a lathe's axis or a mirror's plane: it is named for the kind that can take its place ("a line or a normal", "a workplane or a planar face") and taken from the selection, and the group comes back. It could be given only in the file's text. Scripts: `Features.MendLeftOut`, as for the other entities a file lacks.
- Fillet and chamfer of a crease that fades out part way along its edge and turns back past there, as a loft's crease from a corner of its square foot does above its first ring, round the part past the fade too: a second fillet widens from nothing where the first narrowed to nothing, and narrows to nothing where the crease fades out again. The crease was left sharp there.
- Sweep Along Path round a closed path with a corner beside an arc too sharp for the kernel to join, as a bar round a line meeting an arc at a corner of 170°, fills the outside of the corner as along an open path, going round from the profile and back to it. It was refused, naming the corner radius; where the pieces' outer sides never meet past the corner, it still is.
- Fillet and chamfer of one of a saddle's four edges at its middle, where two ridges and two valleys meet, the others left sharp: the blend runs on into the two faces across the middle and ends where its surface meets their planes, as OpenCASCADE's does. Two opposite edges, the saddle's two ridges or its two valleys, meet in a mitre in the plane of the two left sharp. Both were refused as fillets meeting above an edge left sharp, or as an end where four edges meet. A ridge and a valley side by side are made too, since: each runs up to the sharp edge beside it and patches fill the corner between, the two sharp edges staying sharp. Two opposite edges at different radii or distances are made too: each ends in the plane of the two left sharp, and the plane between their ends is a flat step; they were refused.
- Fillet and chamfer of an edge whose ends both run off a face onto two slopes that meet past it, so large that the blend reaches over that face all along, as on a short plate with a gabled top standing on a rod: the blend takes the face whole, both slopes cutting it and growing over it up to the ridge's line carried on. It was refused as running off "past the next one too", and where the blend's end reached above the far slope carried on past the ridge, as "at both its ends". A blend whose end runs off onto a curved face from a curved end face, the two meeting in a curve, is made too; it was refused.
- Sweep Along Path round a closed path, or along a fitted one (twisted, held to a direction or round a cubic), whose path comes back within the profile's reach of where it has been is made in stretches each clear of itself, joined, as one along open lines and arcs is: a ring whose sides pass nearer each other than the profile is wide becomes one solid. It was refused as passing through itself. Where the stretches don't join (a round profile held to a direction, a square held with its sides in one plane, a twisted ring), it is still refused, saying so; a fitted sweep that failed to close where its passes met now says it passes through itself.
- Features > Broken References mends more of what a damaged file left out: a constraint's line, circle or face the file lacks is named for the kind it must be and taken from the selection, and a group the file lacks (a request's or a constraint's group, or a feature's operand such as an extrusion's sketch) is given by [choose a group], which lists the model's groups. Scripts: `Features.LeftOutMissingKind`, `Features.LeftOutMissingGroups`, `Features.MendLeftOutGroup`
- A fillet or chamfer round a rod through a plate whose foot just reaches the plate's sides, as a fillet of 2 round a rod of radius 4 in a plate 12 wide, is made: the plate's top is pinched to a point at each side, two faces touching there. It was refused within about a hundredth of that size; a fillet whose foot runs only just past a side is made too, at the nearest size within the chord tolerance that the kernel can cut, and says so: the group is marked "(note)" and its screen and `Group.Note()` give the radius made (it was refused from 2.0002 to 2.0028).
- Fillet rounds three of the four edges at the middle of a saddle, where two ridges and two valleys meet, with the fourth left sharp: the two fillets beside the sharp edge run up to it, and patches fill the corner between the three. It was refused. Steep saddles are made, and so are fillets beside the sharp edge of different radii, which reach it at two points: the smaller stops short of the edge.
- Chamfer cuts three of the four edges at the middle of a saddle, with the fourth left sharp, as Fillet rounds them: the two chamfers beside the sharp edge are cut up to it, and patches fill the corner between the three. It was refused. Chamfers beside the sharp edge of different distances, which reach it at two points, are made, and so are steep saddles chamfered at one distance, cut in two goes, the ridges and the valleys apart.
- Sweep Along Path twists a profile along a path of arcs about sharp corners, with no line to twist on: each arc is held still as far as its corner's joint reaches, and the twist turns along the rest of the arcs. Where the joints take the arcs whole, the corner is still a problem on the group naming the corner radius.
- In the browser, Script > Allow AI Connections lets a program in the browser work on the drawing through the page's postMessage: a script of the page's own site, or a page of another site that the user allows in a dialog. The site serves `agent-client.js` for such programs (doc/guide/ai.md, "In a browser").
- Sweep Along Path takes a sharp corner next to a cubic, such as a bar up a line and off at a right angle along a curve, on an open path with no twist, held frames or corner radius: the line and the cubic are each swept, and the outside of the turn is filled where the two, carried on along their tangents, overlap; with the cubic straight that is the mitre two lines make. Where the pieces don't join into a closed solid, as along a round profile, the corner is a problem on the group saying to round it in the path.
- In the browser, the page also draws while a feature does its own work: a fillet's rolling ball and the check of its result, a shell's offsets, a sweep, a loft, the mesh of a solid and its outlines; no test model holds it over a tenth of a second at a time.
- In the browser, the page also draws, and the box counts on, while one group's Boolean runs, such as a thread's union with its blank, which held the page for its length, seconds on a bolt.
- In the browser, a model that takes long to regenerate shows a box saying how long it has taken, and the page draws between its groups: it held the page with nothing shown until the end, so that the browser could call the page unresponsive.
- In the browser, Ctrl+Alt+N, Ctrl+Alt+T and the same with Shift run New, Paste Transformed, Show Naked Edges and Trace Point, whose Ctrl keys the browser keeps for itself; full screen in Chrome or Edge, the Ctrl keys work as they are.
- Sweep Along Path takes a sharp corner beside an arc where the line's side passes outside the arc's, such as a bar 2 wide up a line and back down at 150° onto an arc of radius 12: the line and the arc are swept each on its own and the outside of the turn is filled by where they overlap carried on past the corner, where it was a problem asking for a corner radius. A corner whose pieces' outer sides never meet, the arc turning against it, is still a problem, saying so.
- Shell Solid opens a lathe's or revolve's curved face where it meets a flat face at an inside corner, as a stepped shaft's narrower part meets the step: the inside steps along the flat face's plane round the corner, so the wall under the step keeps its thickness. A curved or sloping face beside the corner, or a step narrower than the thickness, is refused with a message saying so.
- Sweep Along Path takes a closed path that leaves a plane and turns sharply at its corners, such as four lines in 3d. Round such a path the profile would come back turned; the sweep now takes that turn out along the path, held still beside each corner, and mitres the corners, joins those beside an arc in its plane, or rounds them by a corner radius. Before, such a path was refused. Example: `examples/scripts/skewframe`.
- Shell Solid hollows a union with a revolve of more than half a turn whose end lies inside another part, such as a ring revolved three quarters of a turn whose end stands on a block's side, and a half turn whose two ends are hollowed unlike, one inside another part, taken away or a wall and the other not. The end inside the part is moved out into it; an end that is a wall keeps the thickness, running on round the axis. Both were refused.
- A twisted Sweep Along Path takes a sharp corner beside an arc, such as a hook up a line and round an arc: the line and the arc about the corner are joined as on an untwisted sweep, the twist held still there, and the line past the joint's reach takes the twist. Before, the corner was a problem asking for a corner radius. A line too short to leave anything past the joint still is.

### Changed
- An assembly of many linked parts regenerates faster: joining the parts' solids found each curve's handle by a scan of every curve before it; ten links of a plate with 96 holes went from 78 ms to 17 ms. Its display mesh mends each part's triangles alone, rather than every part's against all the others', which a drag of ten touching parts paid at every frame: 177 ms of painting a frame to 118. While a part is dragged, an assembly of linked parts is drawn from each part's own mesh moved where its link puts it, meshed again when the drag ends: a frame of the ten-part drag paints in 18 ms.
- The browser version's download is about 0.1 MB smaller: the STEP files' tessellations are kept in a format of TeleSAPR's own, in place of Go's gob. Tessellations kept by an earlier version (in the cache folder, or in a packed model) are not read; each STEP file is tessellated again once.
- A B1.9 thread's rounded root is made at any chord tolerance: where the model's is coarser than the root's small arcs take, the thread and the part it is made in, every group before it, are made at a tolerance of their own, about a sixth of the arcs' radius, and the groups after it at the model's. It was refused coarser than a fifth of the arcs' radius, the message naming the tolerance to set.
- Curved faces are meshed by the constrained Delaunay mesher in place of the ear clipper, in the display, STL and the other mesh exports: their triangles keep within the chord tolerance, where a sixth of the ear clipper's strayed past it, at about 1.4 times the triangles. A twisted sweep's mesh holds its volume within about 0.05%, where it was off by up to 2%. Flat faces are meshed as before, and a curved face whose Delaunay mesh would cross a neighbour's, as where two faces come within the tolerance of each other, by the ear clipper. Its sag is measured square to its triangles, so a cone or a frustum is not cut finer than it bends (a band on a frustum at a chord of 0.00085 mm took 40 084 triangles, now 6086), and a face that would still take far more points than the ear clipper's grid is meshed by the ear clipper.
- A damaged file's chamfer, fillet or sweep keeps the edges the file names and doesn't hold: the group goes without them and says so, and a save writes them back with their own distances, where a save dropped them. What a damaged file's load leaves out can be mended in the model: Features > Broken References names what the file lacks, and the entity selected takes its place (`Features.LeftOutMissing`, `Features.MendLeftOut`).
- What a damaged file's load leaves out, because it uses something the file lacks, is kept in the file: a save writes it back, to be left out again, so that the file can be mended by hand and opened whole; Broken References lists it and can discard it (`Features.LeftOut`, `Features.DiscardLeftOut`). A save dropped it.
- A click near a line or a point that the solid hides picks the face in front of it, which is what you see, rather than the hidden line: hidden lines were picked through the solid within 10 pixels. With hidden lines drawn as visible lines (View > Show) they are picked as before.

### Fixed
- File > Export > Script (.go), and `Doc.Script`, name a linked part's edges and faces by the linked file's own handles: a part whose entities are copies made by its own groups, as a shelled box's edges, was named through this model's groups of the same numbers, and the script failed to run.
- A Boolean on a solid made by an earlier one whose faces met and cancelled, as a disc drafted both ways from its sketch (two halves joined, their caps on the sketch's plane gone), left the mesh open along that seam, far from what it cut, from a draft of about 8°: even a small hole cut in the middle of such a disc came out unsound with no word on the group.
- A part whose round faces lie closer to the solid's than the chord tolerance but further than a tenth of it, as a pin drawn 0.03 mm over a bore's size at a tolerance of 0.1 mm, or a cone a little off a countersink of its angle, is joined with it: the Boolean failed, or left the mesh open with nothing said, in 174 of 576 such cases tried. Where the Boolean as drawn fails or comes out open, the round faces are moved onto the solid's, and the group says by how much in a note ("(note)" in the list of groups, `Group.Note()` in scripts); where that does not close it either, the Boolean fails.
- Shell hollows a two-sided drafted extrusion with round sides, as a block with a round lobe drafted both ways from its sketch: it was refused as not a shell of any kind it takes. Each half of a side, above and below the neutral plane, is a face of its own, and one can be opened with the other kept. Steep drafts with round sides kept are hollowed too (a disc drafted 20° both ways): the shell came out open along the neutral plane with no word on the group.
- Booleans of two cylinders of one radius whose axes cross, a tee or a cross of equal pipes, are made: they failed whatever the operation, saying the Boolean failed. The two meet in ellipses crossing where the pipes touch, now drawn exact. A tee's difference at a slant whose mesh still crosses itself where the pipes touch is refused, as before.
- A Boolean of a cylinder lying on a block, or a ball resting on one, sunk or standing a hair (under a tenth of the chord tolerance) from touching it, is made: the second solid is moved the hair until the two touch. It failed where the cylinder sank a hair into the block, and could fail for the ball. A union of the two touching along a line is two solids joined there, as Check Solid says.
- A chamfered nut with B1.9 rounded roots and a fine pitch for its size, as a 1/2-20, 1-12 or 4-4 BUTT nut, is sound: the boring's own cone at the chamfered end, which the chamfer takes away, is shallower in such a nut. It came out unsound at every chord tolerance, the window saying so. A 2-8 BUTT nut, which still did, is made sound by an eighth way of placing its ridges' ends, tried last.
- A ball united with, or cut from, a block with a seat of the ball's radius in it is sound: the seat's rim, where a plane meets a sphere, is now an exact circle, the same in every Boolean that meets it, and the faces either side are classified coincident there. Half such Booleans left the rim open, as a solid with naked edges.
- Sweep Along Path with the profile drawn across an open path partway along it, not at an end, sweeps the whole path through the profile where it is drawn. The profile was moved as if drawn at the path's start, so the solid came out offset along the path, past its end and short of its start; a profile drawn across the far end of a path whose ends both lie in its plane was swept from the other end the same way. A profile drawn across a sharp corner of the path is a problem on the group, saying so.
- Opening a damaged file says its left-out items are listed under Features > Broken References, where the entry is; it named the Edit menu, as did the script help of `Features.BrokenReferences`.
- Sweep Along Path no longer stops with an internal error on a path of two arcs alike about a sharp corner whose sweep comes back across itself: the path was cut in two exactly at the corner, leaving no piece on one side of it; it is cut inside an arc instead, or the sweep is a problem on the group.
- The browser version's download is 1.2 MB smaller (39 MB, 11.5 MB compressed): it leaves out the network package, which only the AI connections' socket used, which a browser can't have. Script > Allow AI Connections there seemed to turn on with nothing able to connect; it now takes connections through the page (see Added).
- In the browser, saving under the name of a file already stored asks before replacing it, and File > Exit offers Start Again, which loads the page afresh, where the page had to be reloaded by hand.
- A chamfer or fillet whose foot runs along a rod within a sliver of a line of the rod's mesh, as a chamfer of 1.5 where a plate 2 wide stands on a rod of radius 5 at 50 segments a curve or more, is made and sound: the rod's mesh lay over the chamfer's there and left 83 edges open.
- A fillet or chamfer whose result would not be a closed solid, where the solid before it was one, is refused with a problem on the group that says so (make it a little smaller or larger), where it could be made with its mesh open and nothing said.
- A shell of a block with a round post sunk into its top just beside its side, standing out of it, is made: the post's foot, moved down to join its inside to the block's, took that inside to the block's side below where the post stands out, and the shell was refused. Where a part's inside touches another's along a line, which is no closed solid, the refusal says so and what to change.
- A pin in a bore of its size, as a cylinder extruded into a hole drilled with the same circle or one a hair larger or smaller, joins, cuts and intersects: the sides that lie on one another were taken for one inside the other, and every Boolean failed or left the mesh open.
- A cone in a countersink of its angle, touching it or a hair larger or smaller, joins, cuts and intersects: every Boolean failed, and at exact contact the union was left open round the countersink's rim. A ball in a seat of its radius a hair off is taken onto the seat as the cone is, though some such joins and cuts still leave the mesh open at the seat's rim.
- Analyze > Check Solid, and a script's `Analyze().Check()`, say where a group's part and the solid before it touch only at a point or along a line, as a ball resting on a plate or a rod lying on one, and that the solid is not closed there: it reported only naked or self-intersecting edges, as for a Boolean that went wrong.
- Solids whose flat faces lie a hair apart, closer than a tenth of the chord tolerance, as a block standing a few thousandths of a millimetre off another or sunk that far into it, are joined, cut and intersected as though the faces touched: the Boolean failed, left the mesh open, or came out off in volume by a sliver.
- A turned solid with a curved side beside another whose face crosses that side's surface past its end, as a cylinder 4 mm above a block, both turned, unites with it: the Boolean took the crossing for an edge and failed.
- Sweep Along Path whose path comes back within the profile's reach of where it has been, such as an arc past a sharp corner turning back over the line before it, makes the solid the profile passes through, its overlapping passes joined: it was made crossing itself, with no warning. Where that can't be done (passes that don't join), it is a problem on the group that says the sweep passes through itself, with no solid.
- A fillet whose end only just runs off onto a curved face, as a band's side filleted on a cone past the band's rounded top, is made: it was refused ("only just runs off") within one chord tolerance of the top's edge, or, crossing the top just short of the band's side, as a fillet that takes material away; and a little further off it was made with the sliver the top grew by left open. Its end is now taken to the corner of the side and the top.
- A fillet that runs off onto a curved face is sound at sizes where it was made with 4 naked edges, as a band's side on a cone filleted at 1.725 to 1.8 past a rounded top at 20: the two faces meshed the curve across the fillet 1e-6 apart. That curve now keeps within 1e-7 of both surfaces where 64 cubics can follow it.
- A fillet or chamfer that runs off faces at both its ends, as a chamfer of a short plate with a gabled top where it stands on a rod, past both slopes of the top, is made where the two ends' cuts lie apart along the edge: it was refused as running off "at both its ends" when the two cuts fell on one patch of its surface.
- Analyze > Ray-Cast Check measures a solid whose faces lie square to the axes, as most do, within what its grids agree: each ray crossed its cell through the middle, so the cells beside such a face all fell on one side of it, and a sweep round a closed skew path came out 0.27% large, or the grids never agreed. The rays now cross the cells at spread places.
- A chamfered thread comes out sound at a coarse chord tolerance: its groups are made at a finer tolerance of their own, a twentieth of the pitch or a tenth of a rounded crest's radius, where the model's is coarser; the rest of the part keeps the model's. Buttress nuts were unsound from 0.2%, and ISO, UNC and trapezoidal nuts from 0.5% or 1%, after every way of making the chamfer.
- A buttress nut without a chamfer, as a 1-12 or a 2-8 BUTT nut 20 mm long, comes out sound at a coarse chord tolerance such as 1%, where its thread's Boolean failed and the window said the solid had naked edges: it is made at the thread's own tolerance, a twentieth of the pitch, as a chamfered thread is.
- A chamfered thread as short as its two chamfers allow, as a Tr40x7 nut 16 mm long, has its ends cut: the two cuts crossed in their sketch and cut nothing, leaving the ridges' ends standing past the part's ends, and the thread was reported sound.

- On a group's screen, an edge of the solid named by the faces it lies between (a chamfer's, a fillet's or a sweep's picked on the model) and a face named by its identity (a shell's, a sketch on a face's, a wrap's) are hovered and selected from their rows, as an entity's row does, on the solid before the group, where the edge still is.
- What a feature is built from can be changed after it is made, without making it again: a fillet's or chamfer's edges, a shell's faces, a sweep's or pattern's path, a loft's profiles and a mirror's plane. On the group's screen, [change] takes the selection as the new inputs (with nothing selected it activates the group before the feature, whose solid the inputs are picked on, and the screen stays while they are) and [remove] on a row takes that one away; in scripts, `SetEdges`, `SetFaces`, `SetPath`, `SetProfiles` and `SetPlane` on the typed group. A change the kernel can't make is refused and nothing moves; a later group built on what the change takes away reports it, as when a group is deleted.

- `telesapr-cli export-section` writes the section of the solid by the plane `--plane` names, `z=15` for a plane square to an axis or six numbers for a point and a normal, or by the workplane active when the model was saved, in any 2d vector format.
- A closed loft can be held square to its profiles between the first and the last, as an open loft is: the ring runs straight through each held profile. Before, a closed loft with a profile held square was refused.
- A closed loft can be held by guide curves, each going round the ring through every profile and closing on itself: Features > Guide Loft by Selected Curves on a closed loft, or `LoftGroup.SetGuides`. Before, a closed loft with guides was refused.
- A loft, open or closed, can be held by guide curves and square to its profiles between the ends at once: the guides move the sections of the loft held square, which still leaves each held profile square to it. Before, a loft held both ways was refused ("a loft held by guides can't also be held square to a profile between its ends").

### Changed
- Curved Booleans, threads above all, regenerate about 5% faster: the search for a point's nearest point on a surface stops once its steps repeat. The solids are the same to the bit.
- A datum on a face or an edge of the solid follows its face as the model changes even when its group comes after the active one: a mate, or a model linking the part, finds it where the face is. Before, it stayed where it was until its group was active or the model was saved, which the datums screen noted under its row.
- A convex and a concave chamfer that meet above an edge left sharp, as a ledge's top edge with the inside upright below it or a block's upright with one of its foot edges, meet in one face: the chamfer that reaches the sharp edge runs to it, the other is cut square, and a three-sided face fills the corner, curving on the face the sharp edge bounds from one chamfer's line round onto the edge, as OpenCASCADE's makeChamfer does. Before, the convex chamfer was made first and its plane ran on over the concave one's fill.
- A loft closed into a ring keeps its profiles' size between them: its sections there are turned with the ring, as a closed sweep's are, so a ring of four circles is a torus within 0.2% of its volume where it was 5% short and thinner between its profiles. A ring of three profiles, a third of a turn apart, is made; before, profiles more than a quarter turn apart were refused. Rings have more patches than before.
- A modelled thread with a rounded crest can be chamfered, at either end or both, as a standard or truncated one; before, it was refused ("a rounded crest can't be chamfered"). Scripts lose `sketch.ErrChamferRounded` and gain `sketch.ErrChamferTooFine`, the refusal of a chamfer on a screw so coarse for its size that the cone would reach its axis.
- Thread tolerance classes follow the standards' tables: ISO 965-1's for metric threads and ISO 2903's for trapezoidal ones, looked up by diameter range rather than computed from the size, and ASME B1.1's length of engagement and rounding for unified ones. A 1/4-20 UNC 2A screw is now 0.0037 inch on its pitch diameter, as the table has it, not 0.0040, and an M10 6H nut 180 µm, not 175; a thread made with a class keeps its old clearance until it is edited.
- An ACME thread finer than 10 threads per inch has half the crest clearance, 0.005 inch a side, as ASME B1.5 gives it.

### Removed
- Scripting: `srf.SetChordTolMm`, `srf.SetMaxSegments` and `srf.SetMesher`, which set the tolerance of shells built with none and were meant for tests; a document's chord tolerance and segments are set with `Edit.SetChordTolerance` and `Edit.SetMaxSegments`.

### Fixed
- The configuration screen's redundant constraint timeout is what the search for the constraints to remove takes. Before, the setting was kept but the search always stopped after a second.
- A coarse buttress nut, as a 2-8 BUTT nut, came out with naked edges at chord tolerances of 0.2% and coarser: its ridges reach past the bore by only a tenth of a millimetre, less than the chords along their foot sag, which then dipped into the bore. A nut's ridges whose chords would dip are now swept at half that reach; other threads keep their meshes.
- A B1.9 buttress screw's load flank came out the wrong way round for `PUSH-BUTT` and `PULL-BUTT`: the standard names the way by the flank that leads as screw and nut are put together, the load flank pushing and the clearance flank pulling, on the screw and the nut alike. Nuts were right; screws were turned. A plain `BUTT` now means pulling, as B1.9 has it, and the thread's screen and scripts write the way back (`PUSH-BUTT` or `BUTT`); `PULL-BUTT` is read as `BUTT`. Threads in files keep their ridges.
- A chamfer of a band's side on a cone, the band raised off the cone's base and its chamfer running off onto the band's top, was left open (naked edges) at sizes 2.5 and 3: the chamfer's edge on the cone ran back on itself near where the end sections begin. Where a fillet's or chamfer's sides would run back so, its end sections now leave the rest at an even pace, and the shell is closed.
- A fillet whose foot runs along a curved face close beside a row of the face's mesh, as a fillet of 2 between a plate and a rod of radius 8 it stands on, had its mesh cross itself at some chord tolerances. A curved face meshed into long slivers that cross the faces beyond is now meshed again by the Delaunay mesher.
- A pocket cut from a face by a cutter extruded from that face, flush with it, as a round pocket extruded up from a block's bottom, left any later cut or join with naked edges round the pocket's mouth at fine chord tolerances (0.005 mm and below). The pocket's side and the face now share one edge there.
- A band or rib joined to a cone or lathed part, its end face cutting into the cone's side, failed to join where its top dipped into the cone just past the end face ("the Boolean failed"), and on a lathe whose flat end is made of four patches its bottom edge across that end was left with naked edges where it crosses the seam between them. Both join soundly.
- Analyze > Clearance and Walls measures each solid of a part alone, as two solids of a linked part standing one on the other, whose walls had thousands of points unmeasured where a ray met the other solid; and a hollow inside a solid is part of that solid, not a gap to it in the clearances within parts.
- A rib rounded all over, its slope leaning back, had its mesh cross itself at the front corners at fine chord tolerances, where the foot's fillet passes from the slope edge's fillet onto the slope. A flat face meshed into slivers along a straight trim that cross the face's own edges is now meshed again by the Delaunay mesher.
- A chamfer along a curved edge whose end section, at a flat face, had to be fitted to where the face cuts it was refused with "the fillet's surface can't be fitted", as a chamfer of a band's side on a cone ending on the band's rounded top. The chamfer's surface near that end is now checked against its own rulings, which lean out of the face's plane, rather than against the straight line between its edges, and such chamfers are made.
- Chamfered threads at the coarsest chord tolerances come out sound: a 1/2 BSF nut, and threads with rounded crests (an M8 nut, a 1/2 BSF screw, tapped holes), were left with mesh edges crossing where a ridge's foot crosses the circle at which the bore or core turns into the chamfer's cone. Where two edges of a solid leave a corner nearly along each other on one face, their segments there are now made short enough not to pass through the face beside them.
- A shell of a solid joined from parts, as a plate with a post sunk part way into it, is refused where the post's foot, moved out to meet the plate's inside, would leave the wall below it thinner than the thickness; before, the shell was made with that thinner wall.
- Analyze > Clearance and Walls finds a wall thinner than the points it measures about it, as under the tip of a narrow V-groove (0.2 thick where the points about it measured 0.65): the corners of where the triangles a ray joined cover each other are measured too. A sharp edge, as where a thread's ridge runs out of an end face, measures as thin as nothing there.
- "Solids within parts" takes a part linked as a mesh by its pieces, the triangles joined by the corners they share, and lists the gaps between them, as it does a shell's solids. Before, a mesh was one part.
- A DIN 513 buttress nut (S20x4) at a coarse chord tolerance, 0.4% or 0.5%, is a sound solid: where its ridges met the bore, two vertices of the cut a millionth of a millimetre apart were meshed as two points and left four edges open.
- A corner where fillets of very different radii meet, as 3 beside 0.5 at a box's corner, is filled; before, with radii five or six to one side by side, it was refused as "the patches that would fill it fold". Corners of radii twelve to one apart are filled and tangent all round.
- A fillet of a rim that meets itself in a mitre at a sharp crease of its wall, as round the top of an extruded blob, is made at chord tolerances down to 0.0005%; before, below about 0.005% it was refused with "the fillet's sections near its mitre can't be fitted".
- A chamfered thread whose chamfer's cone meets a ridge just past its crest, as on an M8 stud 30 mm long chamfered at both ends, is sound; before, it came out with open edges at every way of placing its ridges. Where an intersection leaves an edge of a face at a grazing angle, the kernel now follows it the other way when the first way finds nothing.
- File > Export > 2d Section through a plane that cuts the solid, rather than lying along one of its faces, writes where the plane cuts it, where before it wrote an empty file; a plane that misses the solid says so and writes nothing. The section is of the solid as it is at the active group, a sketch drawn after it included.
- A corner where three chamfered edges of one distance meet is cut by a plane through the points where the chamfers' lines cross, as other CAD programs cut it, where before it was a patch of a sphere through those points, bulging out of the plane; and at such a corner whose faces don't all meet square, one chamfer ends between where its lines cross the others' and the other two are cut square to their edges, the corner filled by a four-sided patch, as OpenCASCADE's chamfers are, where before the chamfers ended where a sphere would touch the faces.
- Chamfers of edges that meet at a sharp corner of a curved wall, as a rim's edges between the corners of a profile of curves, or round to their own start on a wall with one crease, or an edge with the one it meets at a corner whose third edge stays sharp, are mitred there as fillets are, where before they were refused as "not mitred". A chamfer that ends on a curved face beside its edge's end is cut at its distance from where the faces meet, where it was up to 0.05 mm off; and chamfers along curved faces at fine chord tolerances are no longer refused as "can't be fitted" where the surface was right.
- Fillets of some of a rim's edges that turn at sharp corners of a curved wall, two or three of four cubics' top edges picked together, are made, mitred where the picked edges meet and ending on the unpicked edges' walls, where before they were refused as "can't be fitted" at fine chord tolerances.
- A screw thread made on a face over its full length follows the face when the part changes afterwards: a rod made longer by a parameter, or a hole drilled deeper, carries a thread as long as it is at the next regeneration, where before the thread kept its old length until it was edited. A face come to reach the part's end, or to stop short of it, or too short for the thread, is reported on the thread's group, and the thread takes it up when edited.
- A menu opened from the keyboard (Alt and a letter, F10, Left and Right) on a very busy machine keeps its first entry active, so Return runs it, instead of sometimes closing without running anything.
- Text drawn in the vector font on two threads at once, as a drawing exported by a script while the window draws, no longer races on the font's glyphs.
- Scripting: `Solid.FaceAt` finds the face of a point on the seam between two quarters of a round face, as on a hole's wall at 0 or 90 degrees, instead of failing with "no face of the solid passes through".

## [0.5.3] - 2026-10-25

### Changed
- Scripting: `Style.SetWidthAs` converts the width and the stipple width to the new unit, as the styles screen does, so the lines keep their look; a script sets the unit before the numbers in it.

### Fixed
- Line styles: freehand lines are drawn as a wave and zigzag lines as straight runs broken by a zigzag, as the styles screen shows them, instead of as short dashes and not at all. A dashed line whose stipple width is zero or too small for its dashes to be a pixel apart is drawn continuous instead of not at all.

- Line styles: the dots of the dot, dash-dot and dash-dot-dot patterns are as wide as the line, rather than a fraction of a pixel and nearly invisible.
- Deleting a line style on the styles screen redraws what was drawn in it at once.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-10-24

### Added
- Wrap Sketch Onto Face: emboss or engrave a sketch (text, shapes) on a cylinder, cone or turned face.
- Closed lofts, lofts held square to a middle profile, and lofts that leave a profile along the sides of the solid it is drawn on.
- Datums: named datums on any face or edge of a solid, found through links as `group.name`, and mates on them.
- Mate Screw (a screw mate by a turn about an axis), Analyze > Clearance and Walls (clearance between parts, wall thickness), density per group and mass properties per part.
- Thread run-out and level starts, threads edited as one block on their helix group, and screw threads drawn by the drafting convention in drawings.
- Shell for lathes and revolves; extrusion depth and revolve angle held by a value; closed sweeps.
- glTF (`.glb`) export, File > Export > Script (the model as the script that builds it), and relink for linked parts.
- Scripting: forms a script declares, filled in a dialog, by `--set` or by MCP; a script library folder; file guard for AI programs; new MCP tools (measure, export, open, list_files).
- Chamfers and fillets onto curved faces by the rolling ball, with fitted end sections.

### Changed
- Faster solving and regeneration: systems solved per connected component and kept between solves, solids kept by what they were made from, a faster software renderer.
- A setting to draw a drag's solids late, off the thread.
- DXF import: arcs in 3D get the normal of their plane.

### Fixed
- Deep wraps onto cones join without hair vertices; a section through a turned solid's axis closes.
- Several fillet, chamfer and sweep cases that failed under the race detector or at twisted patches.
