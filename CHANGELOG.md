# Changelog

What changed in each release of TeleSAPR, newest first. The text of a version's section is the
body of its GitHub release (`make release`, `doc/releasing.md`).

Write an entry in the commit that makes the change, under `## [Unreleased]`, in the group it
belongs to: `Added`, `Changed`, `Fixed` or `Removed`. At the version bump, rename `Unreleased`
to `## [X.Y.Z] - YYYY-MM-DD` and put a new empty `## [Unreleased]` above it.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Help > User's Guide opens the guide in a window of its own, from pages the program carries, so it reads without a network: the site's look, its contents down the left (hidden with ☰ Contents), tables, pictures that follow the window's width up to their own size, the videos played in the page as animations, links followed in the window, Back (Alt+Left), and Open Online for the page on the site. The browser version still opens the site.
- Fillets and chamfers of a pocket's rim wider than its wall is high all along, as in a trough whose top dips lower than the fillet's radius above the floor: the floor cuts the fillet from end to end and the wall goes; refused before ("runs off the face on side 2 all along").
- A linked STEP file's declared volume, surface area and centre (its validation properties) are
  compared with what was read: the link's screen says "volume, area and centre as the file
  declares for 1 part", or, where they differ by more than 1%, names the part and how
  (`g.AsLink().Problem()` in a script).
- A linked STEP solid whose triangles come in open or crossing themselves is said on the link's
  screen ("the mesh of 1 solid has 3 naked edges, so it is not closed"); it came in silently.

### Changed

- The mouse wheel scrolls the guide window and the script console by a steady distance (takigo
  v0.4.11), no longer three lines at a time, so a guide page with pictures no longer jumps a whole
  picture per notch.
- DIN 513 buttress nuts (7H, 8H, 9H) are made with the standard's pitch diameter deviation, 3.1758
  times the axial play 0.1·√P: an S20x4 7H nut has 0.41 mm of clearance, 0.09 mm before.
- ASME B1.9 buttress threads have the sizes and pitches of the standard's Table 1, from 1/2 to 24
  inches, and a size given without its threads per inch takes the middle of the three preferred:
  3/4 BUTT is now 3/4-16 BUTT (12 before) and 1 1/2 BUTT is 1 1/2-10 BUTT (8 before).
- ACME nuts of several starts get the extra clearance of ASME B1.5's Appendix B: 50%, 75% and 100%
  of the class's allowance for two, three, and four or more starts.
- A link's problem on its screen starts "Not all is well with the file:", in place of "Not all
  of the file was read:", since it may now be about what was read.
- A solid with a hollow inside it is exported to STEP as a solid with a void (`BREP_WITH_VOIDS`),
  each cavity a void of the body round it; it was one shell holding both.

### Fixed

- A STEP file's void whose faces were written turned over, bounds and all, as the standard has it,
  came in open where its faces were patches of a sphere.

  packages.

## [0.6.9] - 2026-11-24

### Added
- The Export and import page of the guide has a video of the real window: File > Export > Triangle Mesh, opened from its menu and cancelled.
- Fillets and chamfers whose end runs off both their faces where the two faces past them meet across the fillet's end, as a plate's foot on a cone whose top is low at the end and whose flat comes in close beside the plate: the two faces now grow to meet along the line they share; refused before. Fillets and chamfers that run off both their faces at both ends are made too; refused before.
- Assembly > Mate Ball, Mate Cylindrical, Mate Planar and Mate Slot, and `Assembly.Ball`, `Cylindrical`, `Planar` and `Slot` in a script: a point on a point (the part turns every way about it), an axis on an axis of line segments, circles or datum axes (turns about it and slides along it), a face flat on a face (slides two ways and turns about its normal) and a point on a line (slides along it and turns every way). Each is ordinary constraints in one step of undo, made in 3D.
- A moving part's **freedom** row, and `LinkGroup.Freedom`'s `Text`, name the joint its freedom amounts to (`: a hinge`, `: a ball joint`, `: a slot`, ...); `Freedom` has `Joint`, and a part that turns every way about a point sliding along a line is counted as one (`Invariant` `"line"`).
- Mechanisms: dragging a part held by a driven joint, as the lid on a driven hinge or the engine's piston, moves it by its drive and writes the new value into the drive's parameter (`angle`, `travel`), stopping at the joint's range; it moved nothing before. `Entity.MoveTo` in a script does the same. The drag is one step of undo.
- Motion study of several parameters: Assembly > Motion Study's `[drive another parameter with it]` drives more parameters together, each through its own range over the same steps, as two driven joints of a mechanism; from a script, `doc.Assembly.MotionStudyOf([]api.Drive{...}, steps, frames)`, each frame's `Values` holding every parameter's value. `MotionStudy` stays as it was.
- A motion study saved as one glTF animation: the choice "glTF animation of the whole study", or a `.glb` or `.gltf` file name with no step number from a script, writes a node per linked part and, for each part that moves in a mechanism, a translation and a rotation per step, so that a glTF viewer plays the lid opening or the engine turning. The model must be an assembly of linked parts read as solids.

### Changed
- Small chamfers on curved faces make smaller files and regenerate faster: a chamfer's surface is laid out as far along its edge as the edge and its faces bend, no longer a section every two chamfer distances whatever the edge does. A pen's barrel with the top edges of its mouldings chamfered at 0.2 holds a quarter of the chamfer's patches it did (4816 to 1264), its file goes from 23 MB to 7.1 MB, its STL from 4.5 MB to 1.3 MB and its STEP from 4.9 MB to 2.4 MB, and it regenerates in 2.9 s, where it took 5.3 s.
- STEP export writes AP242 edition 4 in place of AP203: flat faces as planes and round ones as cylinders, cones, spheres and tori, straight and round edges as lines and circles, so the program that reads the file sees a hole as a cylinder of its diameter; each body apart as a solid of its own; each part's volume, surface area and centre as validation properties, for the reader to check what it read; and the part named after the file, with the time it was written. Checked with OpenCASCADE, FreeCAD and NIST's STEP File Analyzer (`scripts/step-exchange.sh`).
- Smaller STEP files: an edge of a face that crosses many patches, as along a long fillet, a sweep, a thread or a chamfer on wrapped mouldings, is written once, as one exact B-spline curve between its two real vertices, where every patch's piece of it was an edge of its own with its own vertices. A bolt's file has half the edges (488 to 240) and is a quarter smaller; the shape and volume read are the same.
- Rounding or chamfering edges that meet in mitres on curved faces is faster again, the same solid to the bit: each edge's own searches now run on several cores, and what they found once is not found again. A pointed arch's foot of ten edges on a tube is rounded in 0.75 s, where it took 1.5 s, and a pen's barrel with the top edges of its four rows of mouldings chamfered regenerates in 4.8 s, where it took 5.9 s.
- Mechanisms assemble from wherever their parts are: each part is put in place from the ground out by its mates, and a loop is closed by searching the turn the parts before it leave free, so a four-bar drawn at full stretch, or with every part on top of the ground where its link put it, now solves, where it didn't. Of two ways to close a loop the one nearest where the parts are is taken, and a motion study keeps to it from step to step; a step of a driven mechanism's motion study is solved without the solver, several times faster to solve. A mechanism with a constraint other than a mate, or a loop through a part held only by points, is solved as before. The engine example starts with its crank turned 60° rather than straight up, where it would now rest at top dead centre.

### Fixed
- A STEP file linked as a mesh, the way a link is read unless it is read as a solid, came in open and too small where a ball's pole lies on an edge of the face round it, as a ball fused at the corner of a box: the ball's face was laid out a turn off past the pole and covered nearly twice over, and nothing said so. It now comes in closed, at its true volume.
- Fillets and chamfers whose foot only just reaches the edge of its face where the next face along the edge begins, as a pocket's rim in a curved top whose fillet is as large as the walls are tall where the top is lowest, were refused ("only just reaches the edge of its face here"); they are now made at the nearest size the kernel can cut, within the chord tolerance, and the group says the size made.
- Mechanisms: a four-bar closed by Mate Concentric on its last pin's holes, its bars lying on top of each other where their links put them, stayed open while its groups said solved: the mate's distances across the axis were made reference dimensions. A mate's constraints are now never made references, so the loop closes whether the parts are set to move before the mates are made or after, and a loop that can't close says it doesn't solve.
- Mechanisms: Mate Hinge on parts not yet set to move, lying on top of each other, kept its point on a point and dropped its point on a line; it keeps both, and setting the parts to move assembles them.
- Mechanisms: a part on a driven hinge where the hinge's angle is a right angle to the frame it is counted in, as a flap shut on its box, read `fixed` in its **freedom** row; it reads a hinge, driven by its parameter. The solver took a coefficient within 0.001 of 0 for 0, and one within 0.001 of 1 for 1, so an equation with such a coefficient, as a driven hinge's within a few degrees of a right angle, was solved a little off what it asks; coefficients are now kept as they are.
- Sweeps with a sharp corner next to a cubic: more of them are made where the path comes back near itself. A closed path whose run round to the cubic passed back by where it started, a twisted closed path whose lines between its corners were too short to hold the twist, a round profile where the path crosses itself, rounded after the cubic, and a twisted diamond at a corner of 30° are now made; a round profile twisted is swept as the same solid untwisted, round a closed path by any turn. Where a line of the path is too short for the mitres at its corners, the problem now says so, not that the corner can't be joined.
- Check Solid and the solid's topology no longer count as a hole of a face one that touches the face's outer edge at a single point: such a face is a disc, as where two equal round tubes cross touching each other, and its Euler count disagreed with the mesh's.
- Scripts: `Entity.MoveTo` and `MoveBy` right after a change, as setting a parameter, moved the points from where they were before the change; they now move them from where the change puts them.

### Changed
- Mechanisms (experimental): turning a part of a mechanism, by dragging one of its points with Shift or Ctrl or by dragging its normal, turns it as its mates let it, the parts joined to it following: a lid on its hinge turns about the hinge, and a lid on a driven hinge turns its `angle` with it, within its bounds. Before, the part was put where it was turned and then solved back. Scripts do the same with the new `Entity.Turn(axis, degrees)` and with `Entity.SetOrientation` on a part's circle.
- Mechanisms (experimental): a large step of a driven joint, as a coarse motion study's, keeps the mechanism as it was put together: a slider-crank driven by its slide no longer flips its crank to the other side of the slide.

### Fixed
- A mate that can't be made from the Assembly menu (or a datum screen's `[mate]`) says why in a message; it made nothing and said nothing.

## [0.6.7] - 2026-11-21

### Added
- AI connection: the model is told when the drawing was changed by someone else since its last call: by you in the window (drawing, deleting, undoing, changing a dimension, dragging, opening another file or starting a new one), or by a second AI program connected to the same window, including while one of the model's scripts runs. The next run_script, get_drawing, render, measure, export or open starts with a line that says so, or that another drawing is open, and run_script's structured result has `drawingChanged`; the model's own scripts, and one taken back, don't count. In a browser, `agent-client.js`'s `call` gives each answer's `revision`. `telesapr-cli mcp` and the window must be the same version (the protocol is now 8).
- The example scripts are built into the program. An AI model asks the MCP server's `api_help` for `examples` to list them, each with what it makes, and for `example bolt` to read one; the primer says so. The script console's `help("examples")` and `help("example bolt")` do the same, in the window and in `telesapr-cli repl`.
- A fillet or chamfer round a rod beside a drafted bore that widens toward the face is made where the bore's cone, carried on past the face, runs into the rod: the rod is kept and the fillet cut back by the cone round it. So is a chamfer or fillet of a hole's rim on a disc standing on a ledge that cuts down past the disc's foot into the ledge, where that cut stays within the disc's side. Both were refused.
- A chamfer or fillet of 3.5 on the rim of a hole near the rim of a drafted disc standing on a ledge is made, where it was refused as running off the face; so is the chamfer of a hole's rim whose cut meets the ledge's top in a circle just touching the disc's foot, made at the nearest size within the chord tolerance at which the two cross, the group saying so. One whose cut comes just inside the foot without meeting it is refused, saying so (make it a little smaller or larger).
- A fillet or chamfer that runs off both its faces at one end and one face at the other is made, as of a plate's foot on a cone where the plate's top is low at both ends and the cone is cut flat past the plate's side at its wide end: each end is cut by the faces past it. It was refused ("runs off both its faces at one end and a face at the other").
- Fillets and chamfers of a pocket's rim turn an inside corner whose crease is curved, as where a wall of a cylinder lying on its side meets a flat wall or another such: the corner between the two is swung about the crease itself. It was refused ("can't yet turn an inside corner whose crease is curved"). This takes in a crease that leans back under the fillet that reaches it first, as where a flat wall meets a leaning one in a sloping face, which was refused as not closed ("make it a little smaller or larger").
- Chamfers and fillets of a pocket's rim edges picked together, turning at the pocket's corners, are made where the chain ends against a wall you didn't pick that leans, as a wall of a cylinder lying on its side in a cone's face: it was refused, "the fillet's surface can't be fitted", where the same edge picked alone was made. So is a chamfer of an edge ending against a flat wall whose curved edge with a leaning wall runs on past the top, which was refused as "part of a longer curve".
- A fillet or chamfer of a pocket's rim whose foot on a wall runs below the wall's foot on the floor where the rim dips lowest, and comes back, is made: the floor cuts it there and grows to the cut, as of a pocket in a lathed dish whose rim edge near the dish's middle is chamfered by 2.25 or more. It was refused as not closed ("make it a little smaller or larger"). One whose foot only just reaches the floor is refused by name ("only just reaches the edge of its face").
- A fillet or chamfer whose edge on a cone or a sphere passes over its tip, as a pocket's rim edge near a cone's apex or near a ball's top, is made. It was refused ("crosses a seam of a face that doesn't reach the edge", or on a ball "the ball the fillet rolls doesn't touch the face"). So is one whose foot passes right through the tip, or within a hair of it either side: it was refused by name ("passes through a pole of the face"), or, just short of the tip, failed as not closed.

- Fillets and chamfers of a pocket's rim turn an inside corner that stands on or near a seam of the face the pocket is cut into, as where a lathed dish's quarters meet, so that the two fillets' feet lie on both sides of the seam. A corner of one radius was refused ("crosses a seam of the face they share"); of two radii or distances the solid failed with "a loop doesn't close". Where one fillet's or chamfer's foot crosses the seam just short of the mitre, as a chamfer of 1 and 1.5 mm can, it is made too, where it was refused ("just short of its end").

- The MCP server offers resources, which a client lets you attach to a message (in Claude Code, `@telesapr:telesapr://drawing`): the drawing in words, as its script and as a picture, one group of it, the scripting primer, and each example script. The `export` tool gives a link to the file it wrote, and says where and how many bytes as data a program can read.
- The MCP server offers prompts, which a client lists as commands (in Claude Code, `/telesapr:draw` and the like): `draw` what you describe, `check` the drawing for what is wrong without changing it, `explain` how it is built, `parametrize` its sizes, and make a drawing `sheet`. Each comes with the drawing as it is, so the model starts at once; with no window running it says why it can't see the drawing. `check`'s group and `sheet`'s paper complete from the drawing's groups and the sheets.

- Help on a type of the scripting interface (`help("ThreadOptions")` in the console, `api_help` over MCP) shows each field with its type and the first sentence of what it is, and help on a field (`help("ThreadOptions.Length")`) all of its comment. Fields were listed by name and type only, and a field asked for by name was not found.

- `Solid.Edge` with a name that is nearly right, as "edge 80030019 80030022" without its ordinal, fails naming the solid's edges between those two faces (or of either face) by their whole names, and says that `get_drawing` with solid lists them and that `EdgeAt(point)` finds an edge with no name. It said only how a name is written.

### Changed
- STEP export (File > Export > Surfaces, `File.Export` to `.step`) warns when it leaves a face out of the file because the curves bounding it don't close, and says where. The face was left out without a word, and other programs read the file as an open shell.
- Menus draw a cascade's arrow and the check and radio marks as shapes, as Tk does, where they were a font's glyphs (takigo 0.4.7).
- File > Export > Script writes a shorter script: a variable that nothing later reads is no longer declared (the requests of a sketch that no constraint refers to, the groups and requests of a thread that the script does not name or use), and a thread's groups get a `SetName` only where you renamed them, not where the thread's call gives the same name. The model it builds is the same.
- Adding a fillet or a chamfer, or changing its edges, is about twice as fast: the edit tried first, to say whether the edges can be rounded, is kept as the group's solid, where the regeneration after made it a second time. Rounding a pointed arch's foot of ten edges on a tube takes 1.5 s, where it took 3.4 s, and making it again 1.5 s, where it took 1.8 s. The solid is the same to the bit.
- Rounding or chamfering edges on curved faces of many patches is faster again, the same solid to the bit: the faces it makes are joined into the solid and the result checked for being closed on several cores, where that was on one. The foot of four rows of Gothic window mouldings on a pen's barrel, 40 edges, is rounded in 5 to 6 s, where it took 13 s, and the barrel with its mouldings' tops chamfered regenerates in 6 s, where it took 10 s. Checking a solid for naked and crossing edges (Analyze) is faster on large meshes too.
- Rounding or chamfering edges that meet in mitres is faster on several cores, the same solid to the bit: the edges of one ring are now made side by side, not one after another. A pointed arch's foot of ten edges on a tube is rounded in 3 s, where it took 5 s, and the top edges of the four rows of mouldings on a pen's barrel are chamfered in 10 s, where they took 12 s.
- The drawing's description (`doc.Describe()`, MCP's `get_drawing`) of a sketch that doesn't solve names the constraints at odds, as `Group.Conflicts` does, where no redundancy names them, and says when the group allows redundant constraints: it said only "NOT SOLVED".
- The drawing's description (`doc.Describe()`, MCP's `get_drawing`) and the status a script run returns no longer make every group after the active one. On a big model whose active group is early, each script and each look at the drawing made the whole model again. A group after the active one that a change left as it was made is now marked "old", as the window's list of groups marks it, and `Group.Old()` says so. A read of the group itself still makes it.

### Fixed
- A picture an AI program takes of a group other than the active one (`render` with `group`) leaves a saved drawing saved. It marked the drawing as changed, and the program's `open` of another file was then refused for unsaved changes there were none of.
- What help says of the scripting interface names calls that exist: `Drawing.Get` pointed at `SetDrawing` for `Drawing.Set`, `AddFaceDimension` at `AddDrawingDimension` for `AddDimension`, `Link` at `LinkInfo` and `LinkProblem` for `g.AsLink().Info()` and `Problem()`, and others; references to the project's roadmap are gone. `Drawing.Export` no longer says it needs the window (it works headless, as over MCP), and `Features.Extrude` says plainly that the depth is left free and how to hold it.
- The primer an AI model reads first (`api_help` with no name) shows `GroupAnalysis.Bounds` and `Doc.Script` taking their two results, as `lo, hi := a.Bounds()`: it showed them as single values, and models printed them beside other values, which Go refuses.
- A STEP file whose curved face's outline reaches the end of its surface at a corner, as a chamfer of a pocket's inside corner in a lathed dish written one surface per face, is read as a solid. It was refused as not closed and read as a mesh, though other programs read it as one valid solid.
- Opening a STEP file as a solid reads a pocket's rim edges rounded whole where they meet at a corner on a curved face, as the files TeleSAPR writes of a lathed dish's pocket, one surface per patch or one per face: they were read as not closed, though other programs read them as one valid solid.
- STEP export writes every face of a solid whose fillet or chamfer ends a few millionths of a millimetre off a straight edge's end, as at a pocket's inside corner rounded in a lathed dish: the walls beside the corner were left out of the file, which other programs then read as an open shell. A hole in a face of a cone or a sphere whose outline reaches the apex or pole, as a pocket cut wholly inside one quarter of a lathed cone, is written as a hole: it was written as a face of its own, and the file read back as a solid and a second, open shell.
- A chamfer or fillet of a hole's rim near the rim of a disc standing on a ledge, whose cut meets the ledge's top in a circle just inside the disc's foot, is made at the size asked for. It was made at a slightly different size, or refused ("only just meets the side's foot"). Cutting, joining or intersecting solids where a circle of one comes within a few thousandths of a millimetre of a circle of the other in the same plane, without touching, no longer fails.
- A fillet or chamfer that runs off its face into a round side, as a hole's rim near a disc's rim on a ledge, is made at a fine chord tolerance, as 0.001%. It was refused as running off the face, though made at the default.
- A fillet of a pocket's rim in a sloping face is made at a fine chord tolerance where the face falls away from a corner of two flat walls along one of its edges, as at a pocket's corner in a cone's face. At 0.002 mm it was refused as not closed ("make it a little smaller or larger"), though made at 0.005 mm and coarser.
- A fillet or chamfer of a hole's rim that runs off the face into a round side is made where the hole comes within half a millimetre of that side, as a hole 0.3 from a disc's rim. It was refused ("runs off the face"). Where its cut goes down past the side's foot into a ledge below and out past the side, the refusal now says so.
- Joining a disc, or a cylinder of any height, with another of the same radius standing in it on its top or its bottom works. The top or bottom lost its outline and the solid was left open.
- Joining, cutting or intersecting two solids whose round faces lie on one another, a cylinder or cone of one on the same cylinder or cone of the other, works when the two faces are divided into pieces at different angles round their axis, as a revolved part divided in its own places against a bore extruded from a circle. The operation failed, or the faces lying on one another lost their outlines and the solid was left open.
- Chamfers that meet at a corner where other edges are left sharp, as two or three of the edges at a pyramid's apex or a pyramid pocket's bottom, give the same solid every time. Their corner came out a little different from one regeneration to the next, in the last digits, so an exported mesh could differ with nothing changed.
- A name the script never declares inside a slice literal inside another literal, as `[]int{1, nope}` or `T{E: []int{nope}}`, is reported with its line and column and as undefined, in the console, `telesapr-cli repl`, `run` and `run_script`. The interpreter panicked while compiling it and the error had no place; a fault of the interpreter whose place is unknown is now at the start of the statement in the console too.
- The console shows the value of an expression that holds a function literal naming its results as `run` makes it: a composite literal that a closure assigns to such a result was shown as its zero value, `{A:0}` for what `run` gave as `{A:1}`.
- File > Export > Script of a model with a thread keeps what you set on the thread's groups: their names, colours, visibility and suppression, which the script lost (the thread's call makes them with the defaults), and it names the lines and points of the thread's sketches that its constraints use as it names others (`g9.Requests()[2].Entity()`, `r16.Part(2)`), where it wrote their handles (`doc.Entity(0x100002)`), which another drawing may number differently.
- Copies of a cut turned 45° apart about an axis its outline touches, five or six of them, are cut from the solid at once too, where their union failed and they were cut one at a time; so are six copies 60° apart whose union failed or not on the last digits of the sketch plane's angle.
- Copies of a cut turned about an axis its outline touches are cut from the solid at once, as a step and repeat cuts any copies, where their union failed and they were cut one at a time: a triquetra drawn in a plane through a tube's axis, one corner on it, turned six times by 60°, regenerates in about half the time. The union of such copies, which meet one another along the same curves in several places (two copies' sides in the plane of a third's cap, the caps of copies half a turn apart face to face), is sound by itself too.
- The column of an error in a script points at the text you wrote when it is in a function literal or in a function that names its results, as `func() int { return nope }()` or `func() (r int) { return 1 }() + nope`, in the console, `telesapr-cli repl` and `run`, and `run_script`. It counted from characters the interpreter and the script engine put before the statement or into it, and came out 14 to 33 columns too far right.
- Fillets of different radii of a pocket's rim in a flat face are made whichever order the edges are picked in. Picked against the way round the rim, as the right side and then the bottom, or 0.5, 1 and 2 mm from the top round to the bottom, they were refused, "an edge at the end of the fillet runs back along it", while the same edges picked the other way were made.
- File > Export > Script of a model with a thread whose groups were moved apart, its lathe moved on its own past a later cut say, makes the thread again as a thread. It was written group by group, its helix as a plain helix, so the drawing the script made had lost the thread: moved back together, its groups were no thread to edit. The script now makes it with its thread call and moves its groups to where they stood, so the drawing made has the same groups in the same order and the same thread. The same holds for a thread with a sketch moved in between its blank sketch and its lathe, whose script failed to run, and for a cosmetic thread whose lathe no longer followed its sketch, whose lathe was made twice. A script reads the circle a thread is on with `ThreadGroup.Circle`.

## [0.6.6] - 2026-11-17

### Added
- Opening a file, saving one and exporting one show a progress window: the file being read when one is opened, each group as it is regenerated with a bar for how far it has got, then the file being written. The dialogs that chose the file and its options close before it comes up, and it closes when the work is done. The window used to stop drawing until the end, so the file dialog stayed painted over it and the program looked hung. In the browser the busy box shows the same, with a bar.

### Changed
- Rounding or chamfering edges on curved faces made of several patches is faster, the same solid to the bit: the top edges of four rows of Gothic window mouldings raised on a pen's barrel are chamfered in 12 s, where they took two and a half minutes (separate rings of edges rounded in one step are now made side by side, on several cores), and a pointed arch's foot is rounded in a third of the time.
- The way a revolve turns is written down, in `Features.Revolve`'s help, the guide and the primer an AI model reads: a positive angle turns the other way to the right-hand rule about the axis line, from its first point to its second. A band revolved from a pocket's edge over MCP went the other way, stayed inside the wall and added nothing, which read as a Boolean that failed silently.

### Fixed
- The window says when a group after the active one is not up to date. It goes with a B1.9 rounded root, a chamfer and a run-out, and a screw mate fits rounded buttress threads as flat ones. Before, a rounded buttress crest was refused ("a buttress thread's crest can't be rounded").
- A twisted sweep may turn at a sharp corner between two lines: the twist is held still along each line beside the corner, over the stretch the corner's mitre spans, and turns along the rest of the path, so the corner is mitred as an untwisted one is. Before, a twisted sweep's corner needed a corner radius.
- A sweep along a cubic, twisted, or held to a direction may turn at a sharp corner beside an arc, in the arc's plane: the lines and arcs about the corner are swept as a sweep along lines and arcs is, joined where each point of the profile's paths along the two pieces cross, and the rest of the path is fitted as before. Held to a direction, it must be the arc's axis; a twist is held still along them. Before, such a corner needed a corner radius.
- A shell hollows more solids joined by unions: a revolve of half a turn or less whose ends stand inside another part, as a half ring on a block's side, has its ends moved out into that part, and a prism whose side lies partly inside another part, as a round or square post sunk into a block's side, moves each side as it lies, in as a wall, out where it is all inside the other part, a round side running on smoothly into a wall moved in with it. Before, both were refused.
- A shell hollows a drafted extrusion joined to other parts, as a drafted round boss on a block, a square post through a drafted cylinder or a drafted pocket cut into a block: each drafted side moves square to itself, in as a wall, out where it lies inside another part or belongs to a part taken away. A drafted extrusion may also be opened at a round side between outside corners, whose inside runs out past it. Before, a drafted extrusion was hollowed only alone and only open at its ends.
- A shell hollows a solid with a revolve of less than a whole turn taken away from it, as a half or a quarter turned down into a block: the cutter grows by the thickness, its ends' planes moved out too, so a wall of the thickness stands round the cut. A face taken away that lies on a lathe or revolve taken away from the solid or intersected with it, as the floor of a turned pocket or the top of a lathe cut by a block, opens the inside through it. Before, both were refused.
- Two fillets of different radii meet at a corner whose third edge is left sharp, as a block's two top edges at 1 and 2 mm above its upright, a ledge's two top edges above the inside upright, or a rib's two foot edges below its upright: the smaller fillet ends on the larger one's surface, along the curve where the two meet, and the larger goes on to the third edge, as OpenCASCADE's makeFillet makes it. Before, the corner was refused ("fillets of radius 1 and 2 meet here above an edge left sharp").
- A solid made of lathes, revolves and extrusions some of which are taken away from it or intersected with it, as a block with a round pocket or a groove lathed into it, or a cone or ball cut flat by a block, is hollowed by a shell: the inside is the parts' insides joined as the parts were, a part taken away grown by the thickness, so a wall of the thickness stands round a pocket and over its floor. A face taken away on a part taken away or intersected with, as a pocket's floor or the flat down a cut cone, opens the inside through it. Before, such solids were refused as curved solids.
- A sweep along a cubic, or with its frames held to a direction, may turn at a sharp corner between two lines: the two straight runs are mitred by the plane halving the corner, as a sweep along lines alone is. Held to a direction, the corner must turn about that direction. A twisted sweep, or one with a corner beside an arc, takes a corner radius and is swept round the corner's arc. Example: `examples/scripts/zigzag`. Before, any corner on such a sweep was refused.
- Fillets meet at more corners where edges that stick out meet edges that go in. Where all three edges at such a corner are rounded and the two of one kind have different radii, as a ledge's two top edges at 1 and 2 mm over the inside upright, the other edge is rounded first and the two go on round the arc it leaves, the radius changing from one's to the other's along it, tangent all round. At a corner of four or more such edges, as the middle of a saddle, all rounded, the fillets are set back and the hole filled by patches. Before, both were refused ("a convex edge's fillet meets a concave one's here").
- Fillets of different radii meet in mitres in more ways: a fillet between two smaller ones of different radii, a fillet between a larger one and a smaller one, and a mitre of one radius beside one of two radii on the same chain, as the top edges of a prism of three curved sides rounded at 0.5, 1 and 0.75. Chamfers of different distances meet in mitres as fillets do. Before, each was refused ("pick it alone").
- A fillet of an edge that sticks out and one of an edge that goes in meet at a corner whose third edge is left sharp, as a ledge's top edge with the inside upright below it or a rib's upright with one of its foot's edges: one patch, tangent to both fillets and to the face, fills the corner, the sharp edge ending in it, as OpenCASCADE's makeFillet does, whichever fillet is the larger and at corners from 60 to 120 degrees. Before, the corner was refused ("a convex edge's fillet meets a concave one's here").
- Analyze > Ray-Cast Check and `group.Analyze().RayCheck()` check sweeps fitted along their path: along a cubic, twisted, held to a direction, or round a closed path out of a plane. The check places the profile along the path by frames of its own and compares the kernel's fitted mesh with it ray by ray. Before, such a sweep was refused as one the check can't rebuild.
- A sweep's path may turn at a corner beside an arc with no corner radius: where a line meets an arc, or two arcs meet, at an angle in the arc's plane, the sweep's sides are joined where each point of the profile's paths along the two pieces cross, the outside of the turn filled by both pieces carried on past it, as a mitre between two lines does. Example: `examples/scripts/hook`. A corner too sharp for the arc beside it, or a piece too short for the joint, is a problem on the group, which a corner radius rounds. Before, every corner beside an arc needed a corner radius.
- A modelled screw thread can have a phase and a run-out together: the ridges are moved along the thread by the phase and still fade out into the run-out's cone. Before, a thread with a run-out refused a phase ("a thread with a run-out can't be phased").
- A solid joined by unions from lathes, revolves and extrusions, as a turned body with an extruded boss, a cup with a lug revolved part of a turn round it, or a block with a round boss, is hollowed by a shell: each part's walls move in, and a part's face lying inside another, as a boss's foot sunk into the body, moves out, so the insides meet with no wall between them. Before, a lathe joined to anything but lathes about its axis, and a block with a round boss, were refused as curved solids.
- A drafted extrusion whose sides are cones, as a drafted cylinder, ring or slot, is hollowed by a shell open at its ends: each side is moved in square to itself, so the walls keep the thickness along the cones, and a two-sided draft's inside leans both ways from its neutral plane. Before, such a shell was refused as a curved solid.
- Chamfers meet at more corners. Where convex and concave edges meet at a corner of three, all chamfered, as at a ledge's top above the concave edge between its walls or at a rib's foot below its upright, the corner is filled by a face as OpenCASCADE's is. Where some edges at a corner of four or more stay sharp, as at a pyramid's apex or the bottom of a pyramid-shaped pocket, the chamfers meet where their planes do. A chamfered edge that ends at the inside corner of an L-shaped face stops square at the next wall's plane. Before, a ledge's corner was refused and a rib's foot was made in stages, unlike OpenCASCADE's; the others were cut by wedges, which went on past an inside corner into the solid behind it, or refused where an edge was concave.
- ASME B1.9 buttress threads can have their roots rounded as the standard draws them: **rounded root** on the thread's screen (`RoundRoot` in scripts) rounds each root by an arc of 0.07141 pitches tangent to both flanks. The arcs are small, and a chord tolerance too coarse for them is refused with the one to set. A B1.9 designation may also say which way the load goes, `1-12 PUSH-BUTT` or `1-12 PULL-BUTT`; PULL-BUTT turns the load flank back as **load flank back** does.
- A chamfer's edges can have distances of their own: each edge's row on the chamfer's screen has [own distance] and [own second distance], and scripts have `ChamferGroup.SetEdgeDistance` and `SetEdgeSecondDistance`. An edge with none takes the chamfer's, and swapping the chamfer's distances swaps each edge's too. Before, a chamfer had one distance and one second distance for all its edges.
- A fillet or chamfer whose end runs off its face onto a curved face, such as a band's rounded top, is made: the curved face is carried on past its edge, cuts the blend and grows over its end. A fillet ending on a curved face that a side's face goes on past, as a cone past a band's rounded top, is made the same way. Before, both were refused.
- Two chamfered edges meet at a corner whose third edge stays sharp with any distances: two distances on each, or a different distance on each edge. The chamfers meet along the line where their planes cross, and the one that reaches further down the sharp edge goes on past it. This works where the chamfers cut (a block's top corner) and where they fill (the foot of a block standing on a plate, whose side face grows out to the mitre), as OpenCASCADE's makeChamfer does.
- A datum on a face neither flat nor round, or on an edge neither straight nor round, is taken where the face or edge was clicked, and follows that place as the model changes, rather than at its middle, keeping its end of an edge when an edit turns the edge round. Scripts name such a place with `Datums.NameFaceAt` and `Datums.NameEdgeAt`.
- A revolve of more than half a turn is hollowed with one or both of its ends kept as walls: the inside keeps the thickness from each end and is rounded about the axis, where the ends meet. Before, such a shell was refused unless both ends were taken away.
- Solids made of lathes about one axis joined to each other, as a cup with a flange round its foot or a cylinder with a groove turned out of it, are hollowed by their profiles joined, so the walls follow every lathe exactly. Before, a shell of a lathe with any other solid was refused as a curved solid.
- Buttress threads: DIN 513's metric S threads (`S20x4`, flanks at 3° and 30°) and ASME B1.9's buttress inch threads (`1-12 BUTT`, 7° and 45°), typed or chosen from two new series, `buttress` and `BUTT`. The ridge leans, its load flank facing along the thread on a screw and back in a nut; **load flank back** on the thread's screen (`LoadFlankBack` in scripts) turns it about, and the screw mate refuses two buttress threads whose load flanks face the same way. A buttress crest can be truncated but not rounded, and buttress threads have no tolerance classes.
- A rib can be rounded all over in one edit: its top's edges and its foot picked together. The top is rounded first and the foot's fillet goes on round onto the top's fillets where they reach the floor. A closed foot left by fillets made before is filled whole too, from its edges or one of them. Before, such picks were refused ("a convex edge's fillet meets a concave one's here", or "pick it alone").
- Chamfers meet at any corner where every edge is chamfered, as OpenCASCADE's do: chamfers of two distances at a corner, three edges whose faces don't meet square, and four or more edges at a pyramid's apex. One chamfer ends across between where its lines cross the others', the others are cut square to their edges, and the corner is filled by a plane or a four-sided patch. Before, such corners were refused by the edit and cut by wedges into a point, or refused ("the edge ends where 4 edges meet"). Refusals at a corner of chamfers now say "chamfer" where they said "fillet".
- Fillets of different radii meet in mitres where their edges meet at a sharp corner of a curved wall, or at a corner whose third edge stays sharp: the smaller fillet ends against the larger one on its own wall, and the larger one's end turns onto that wall down to the corner, as other CAD programs make it. Before, edges of different radii meeting so were refused ("pick it alone").
- On a group's screen, an edge of the solid named by the faces it lies between (a chamfer's, a fillet's or a sweep's picked on the model) and a face named by its identity (a shell's, a sketch on a face's, a wrap's) are hovered and selected from their rows, as an entity's row does, on the solid before the group, where the edge still is.
- What a feature is built from can be changed after it is made, without making it again: a fillet's or chamfer's edges, a shell's faces, a sweep's or pattern's path, a loft's profiles and a mirror's plane. On the group's screen, [change] takes the selection as the new inputs (with nothing selected it activates the group before the feature, whose solid the inputs are picked on, and the screen stays while they are) and [remove] on a row takes that one away; in scripts, `SetEdges`, `SetFaces`, `SetPath`, `SetProfiles` and `SetPlane` on the typed group. A change the kernel can't make is refused and nothing moves; a later group built on what the change takes away reports it, as when a group is deleted.

- `telesapr-cli export-section` writes the section of the solid by the plane `--plane` names, `z=15` for a plane square to an axis or six numbers for a point and a normal, or by the workplane active when the model was saved, in any 2d vector format.
- A closed loft can be held square to its profiles between the first and the last, as an open loft is: the ring runs straight through each held profile. Before, a closed loft with a profile held square was refused.
- A closed loft can be held by guide curves, each going round the ring through every profile and closing on itself: Features > Guide Loft by Selected Curves on a closed loft, or `LoftGroup.SetGuides`. Before, a closed loft with guides was refused.
- A loft, open or closed, can be held by guide curves and square to its profiles between the ends at once: the guides move the sections of the loft held square, which still leaves each held profile square to it. Before, a loft held both ways was refused ("a loft held by guides can't also be held square to a profile between its ends").

### Changed
- Curved Booleans, threads above all, regenerate about 5% faster: the search for a point's nearest point on a surface stops once its steps repeat. The solids are the same to the bit.
- A datum on a face or an edge of the solid follows its face as the model changes even when its group comes after the active one: a mate, or a model linking the part, finds it where the face is. Before, it stayed where it was until its group was active or the model was saved, which the datums screen noted under its row.
- A convex and a concave chamfer that meet above an edge left sharp, as a ledge's top edge with the inside upright below it or a block's upright with one of its foot edges, meet in one face: the chamfer that reaches the sharp edge runs to it, the other is cut square, and a three-sided face fills the corner, curving on the face the sharp edge bounds from one chamfer's line round onto the edge, as OpenCASCADE's makeChamfer does. Before, the convex chamfer was made first and its plane ran on over the concave one's fill.
- A loft closed into a ring keeps its profiles' size between them: its sections there are turned with the ring, as a closed sweep's are, so a ring of four circles is a torus within 0.2% of its volume where it was 5% short and thinner between its profiles. A ring of three profiles, a third of a turn apart, is made; before, profiles more than a quarter turn apart were refused. Rings have more patches than before.
- A modelled thread with a rounded crest can be chamfered, at either end or both, as a standard or truncated one; before, it was refused ("a rounded crest can't be chamfered"). Scripts lose `sketch.ErrChamferRounded` and gain `sketch.ErrChamferTooFine`, the refusal of a chamfer on a screw so coarse for its size that the cone would reach its axis.
- Thread tolerance classes follow the standards' tables: ISO 965-1's for metric threads and ISO 2903's for trapezoidal ones, looked up by diameter range rather than computed from the size, and ASME B1.1's length of engagement and rounding for unified ones. A 1/4-20 UNC 2A screw is now 0.0037 inch on its pitch diameter, as the table has it, not 0.0040, and an M10 6H nut 180 µm, not 175; a thread made with a class keeps its old clearance until it is edited.
- An ACME thread finer than 10 threads per inch has half the crest clearance, 0.005 inch a side, as ASME B1.5 gives it.

### Removed
- Scripting: `srf.SetChordTolMm`, `srf.SetMaxSegments` and `srf.SetMesher`, which set the tolerance of shells built with none and were meant for tests; a document's chord tolerance and segments are set with `Edit.SetChordTolerance` and `Edit.SetMaxSegments`.

### Fixed
- The configuration screen's redundant constraint timeout is what the search for the constraints to remove takes. Before, the setting was kept but the search always stopped after a second.
- A coarse buttress nut, as a 2-8 BUTT nut, came out with naked edges at chord tolerances of 0.2% and coarser: its ridges reach past the bore by only a tenth of a millimetre, less than the chords along their foot sag, which then dipped into the bore. A nut's ridges whose chords would dip are now swept at half that reach; other threads keep their meshes.
- A B1.9 buttress screw's load flank came out the wrong way round for `PUSH-BUTT` and `PULL-BUTT`: the standard names the way by the flank that leads as screw and nut are put together, the load flank pushing and the clearance flank pulling, on the screw and the nut alike. Nuts were right; screws were turned. A plain `BUTT` now means pulling, as B1.9 has it, and the thread's screen and scripts write the way back (`PUSH-BUTT` or `BUTT`); `PULL-BUTT` is read as `BUTT`. Threads in files keep their ridges.
- A chamfer of a band's side on a cone, the band raised off the cone's base and its chamfer running off onto the band's top, was left open (naked edges) at sizes 2.5 and 3: the chamfer's edge on the cone ran back on itself near where the end sections begin. Where a fillet's or chamfer's sides would run back so, its end sections now leave the rest at an even pace, and the shell is closed.
- A fillet whose foot runs along a curved face close beside a row of the face's mesh, as a fillet of 2 between a plate and a rod of radius 8 it stands on, had its mesh cross itself at some chord tolerances. A curved face meshed into long slivers that cross the faces beyond is now meshed again by the Delaunay mesher.
- A pocket cut from a face by a cutter extruded from that face, flush with it, as a round pocket extruded up from a block's bottom, left any later cut or join with naked edges round the pocket's mouth at fine chord tolerances (0.005 mm and below). The pocket's side and the face now share one edge there.
- A band or rib joined to a cone or lathed part, its end face cutting into the cone's side, failed to join where its top dipped into the cone just past the end face ("the Boolean failed"), and on a lathe whose flat end is made of four patches its bottom edge across that end was left with naked edges where it crosses the seam between them. Both join soundly.
- Analyze > Clearance and Walls measures each solid of a part alone, as two solids of a linked part standing one on the other, whose walls had thousands of points unmeasured where a ray met the other solid; and a hollow inside a solid is part of that solid, not a gap to it in the clearances within parts.
- A rib rounded all over, its slope leaning back, had its mesh cross itself at the front corners at fine chord tolerances, where the foot's fillet passes from the slope edge's fillet onto the slope. A flat face meshed into slivers along a straight trim that cross the face's own edges is now meshed again by the Delaunay mesher.
- A chamfer along a curved edge whose end section, at a flat face, had to be fitted to where the face cuts it was refused with "the fillet's surface can't be fitted", as a chamfer of a band's side on a cone ending on the band's rounded top. The chamfer's surface near that end is now checked against its own rulings, which lean out of the face's plane, rather than against the straight line between its edges, and such chamfers are made.
- Chamfered threads at the coarsest chord tolerances come out sound: a 1/2 BSF nut, and threads with rounded crests (an M8 nut, a 1/2 BSF screw, tapped holes), were left with mesh edges crossing where a ridge's foot crosses the circle at which the bore or core turns into the chamfer's cone. Where two edges of a solid leave a corner nearly along each other on one face, their segments there are now made short enough not to pass through the face beside them.
- A shell of a solid joined from parts, as a plate with a post sunk part way into it, is refused where the post's foot, moved out to meet the plate's inside, would leave the wall below it thinner than the thickness; before, the shell was made with that thinner wall.
- Analyze > Clearance and Walls finds a wall thinner than the points it measures about it, as under the tip of a narrow V-groove (0.2 thick where the points about it measured 0.65): the corners of where the triangles a ray joined cover each other are measured too. A sharp edge, as where a thread's ridge runs out of an end face, measures as thin as nothing there.
- "Solids within parts" takes a part linked as a mesh by its pieces, the triangles joined by the corners they share, and lists the gaps between them, as it does a shell's solids. Before, a mesh was one part.
- A DIN 513 buttress nut (S20x4) at a coarse chord tolerance, 0.4% or 0.5%, is a sound solid: where its ridges met the bore, two vertices of the cut a millionth of a millimetre apart were meshed as two points and left four edges open.
- A corner where fillets of very different radii meet, as 3 beside 0.5 at a box's corner, is filled; before, with radii five or six to one side by side, it was refused as "the patches that would fill it fold". Corners of radii twelve to one apart are filled and tangent all round.
- A fillet of a rim that meets itself in a mitre at a sharp crease of its wall, as round the top of an extruded blob, is made at chord tolerances down to 0.0005%; before, below about 0.005% it was refused with "the fillet's sections near its mitre can't be fitted".
- A chamfered thread whose chamfer's cone meets a ridge just past its crest, as on an M8 stud 30 mm long chamfered at both ends, is sound; before, it came out with open edges at every way of placing its ridges. Where an intersection leaves an edge of a face at a grazing angle, the kernel now follows it the other way when the first way finds nothing.
- File > Export > 2d Section through a plane that cuts the solid, rather than lying along one of its faces, writes where the plane cuts it, where before it wrote an empty file; a plane that misses the solid says so and writes nothing. The section is of the solid as it is at the active group, a sketch drawn after it included.
- A corner where three chamfered edges of one distance meet is cut by a plane through the points where the chamfers' lines cross, as other CAD programs cut it, where before it was a patch of a sphere through those points, bulging out of the plane; and at such a corner whose faces don't all meet square, one chamfer ends between where its lines cross the others' and the other two are cut square to their edges, the corner filled by a four-sided patch, as OpenCASCADE's chamfers are, where before the chamfers ended where a sphere would touch the faces.
- Chamfers of edges that meet at a sharp corner of a curved wall, as a rim's edges between the corners of a profile of curves, or round to their own start on a wall with one crease, or an edge with the one it meets at a corner whose third edge stays sharp, are mitred there as fillets are, where before they were refused as "not mitred". A chamfer that ends on a curved face beside its edge's end is cut at its distance from where the faces meet, where it was up to 0.05 mm off; and chamfers along curved faces at fine chord tolerances are no longer refused as "can't be fitted" where the surface was right.
- Fillets of some of a rim's edges that turn at sharp corners of a curved wall, two or three of four cubics' top edges picked together, are made, mitred where the picked edges meet and ending on the unpicked edges' walls, where before they were refused as "can't be fitted" at fine chord tolerances.
- A screw thread made on a face over its full length follows the face when the part changes afterwards: a rod made longer by a parameter, or a hole drilled deeper, carries a thread as long as it is at the next regeneration, where before the thread kept its old length until it was edited. A face come to reach the part's end, or to stop short of it, or too short for the thread, is reported on the thread's group, and the thread takes it up when edited.
- A menu opened from the keyboard (Alt and a letter, F10, Left and Right) on a very busy machine keeps its first entry active, so Return runs it, instead of sometimes closing without running anything.
- Text drawn in the vector font on two threads at once, as a drawing exported by a script while the window draws, no longer races on the font's glyphs.
- Scripting: `Solid.FaceAt` finds the face of a point on the seam between two quarters of a round face, as on a hole's wall at 0 or 90 degrees, instead of failing with "no face of the solid passes through".
